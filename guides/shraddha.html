Educational overview

Shraddha & annual ancestral observances

Tradition variation: Who performs shraddha, which lunar dates matter, whether food offerings are made at home or a temple, and whether Pitru Paksha is observed all differ. Ask your priest or elders.

In many Hindu families, shraddha refers to rites of remembrance for ancestors — often yearly on a death anniversary (tithi), and/or during a broader ancestral fortnight known in many North Indian calendars as Pitru Paksha.

Common patterns (not rules)

  • Annual death anniversary — rites on or near the lunar date of death, led with priestly guidance in many households.
  • Pitru Paksha / similar periods — a multi-day window when families prioritise ancestral offerings; exact calendars depend on region and almanac.
  • Offerings — food, water, sesame, or other items as per custom; some families feed relatives, guests, or those in need as part of the observance.
  • Who participates — traditionally often a son or designated relative; modern families adapt with priestly advice when roles are unclear.

Why families reach out for guidance

Distance, incomplete knowledge of the family calendar, or the first anniversary after a death are common reasons people want a clear outline. DharmaSetu can help you draft next steps; a priest confirms dates and the form of the rite for your lineage.

Honest limits

We do not sell guaranteed “results” for ancestors. Shraddha is described here as a family and community practice. Confirm timing and procedure with someone who knows your tradition. Annual reminders are something we plan to offer later — they are not built on this site yet.
