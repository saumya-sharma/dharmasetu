Shraddha & Pitra Paksha

Tradition variation: Who performs shraddha, which lunar dates matter, whether offerings are made at home or a temple, and how Pitra Paksha is observed all differ. Ask your priest or elders.

In many Hindu families, shraddha refers to rites of remembrance for ancestors — often yearly on a death anniversary (tithi), and during a broader ancestral fortnight known in many North Indian calendars as Pitra Paksha (Pitru Paksha).

Common patterns (not rules)

  • Annual death anniversary — rites on or near the lunar date, often with priestly guidance.
  • Pitra Paksha — a multi-day window when families prioritise ancestral offerings; exact calendars depend on region and almanac.
  • Offerings — food, water, sesame, or other items as per custom; some households also feed guests or those in need.
  • Who participates — traditionally often a designated relative; modern families adapt with priestly advice when roles are unclear.

Why families seek a roadmap

Distance, incomplete knowledge of the family calendar, or the first years of keeping observances alone are common reasons people want a clear outline. DharmaSetu helps you draft next steps for annual care; a priest confirms dates and form for your lineage.

Honest limits

We do not claim spiritual outcomes for any observance. Shraddha and Pitra Paksha are described here as family and community practice. Confirm timing and procedure with someone who knows your tradition.
